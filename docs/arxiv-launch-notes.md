## LinkedIn draft

Two customers ask their AI agents to book a hotel. Both agents choose the same one. One customer cares about quiet; the other needs to be close to a meeting.

What should the company build next: better soundproofing or better transport links?

A completed transaction may not answer that question.

In The Delegation Blind Spot, I study how to audit whether a declared stream of agent observations supports a specific future product decision. The workflow reports compatible decision intervals and shows populations that fit the same evidence but favor different changes.

The result that most changed my thinking was a simple baseline. In our controlled synthetic tasks, preserving supplied structured preferences resolved seven of nine comparisons without model calls. A model-generated receipt resolved three of nine per model. More AI was not automatically better measurement.

The report combines proofs, 7,200 actual model requests across primary and exploratory experiments, 14,400 controlled simulations, and reproducible code. There are no human participants or real customer outcomes. The claims are limited to the declared models and computational setting.

For teams building agent-mediated products, my question is: which evidence would actually change your next product decision, and are you preserving it?
